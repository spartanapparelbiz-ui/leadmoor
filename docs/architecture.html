The brief

One sentence in, a defensible list out

Series B–C fintech companies in the UK and Netherlands, 50–300 employees, that have shipped something with AI in the last six months and are hiring backend engineers. I want the VP Engineering or Head of Platform. Skip anyone already in our CRM. Representative request

Six distinct problems hide in that sentence: parse intent into machine-checkable criteria, discover candidates from permitted sources, resolve them into real companies without merging two different ones, enrich them, score them against criteria that are partly fuzzy, and prove each answer. The architecture below is organised around the observation that only one of those six wants a language model in the critical path.

Load-bearing decisions

Three commitments

Everything else follows from these. Relax any one and the product becomes a plausible-sounding hallucination machine that a customer will catch before we do.

Commitment one

The model never authors a fact

The LLM may do exactly three things: compile the request into a typed spec, propose queries, and judge supplied evidence while citing the spans it used. It is never asked “which companies match this?” and never permitted to emit a company name, a headcount, or an email address from its own weights.

A verdict citing evidence that does not exist — or quoting a span absent from the cited document — is rejected by a validator and marked insufficient_evidence. It fails closed. An unknown is reported as an unknown, never scored as a zero.

Commitment two

A non-deterministic planner over a deterministic substrate

The model's output is a LeadSpec: a versioned, typed, readable document of filters, personas, rubric, and budget. The user reads and edits it before a penny is spent. Execution is then an ordinary deterministic pipeline with no model in the control flow.

This buys reproducible runs, replayable audits, cost ceilings that actually hold, a debuggable failure surface, and cheap re-runs when one weight changes.

Commitment three

Permission is a code path, not a policy document

Every connector ships a manifest declaring legal basis, jurisdiction, permitted field classes, robots posture, rate limits, retention, and redistribution rights. A policy engine evaluates it at three gates: before fetch, after extraction, and at export.

“Permitted public data” has to be something the system enforces, not something the README claims.

Anti-hallucination

Claims, not fields

A company record is not a row of values. It is a set of claims, and the “current” value of any field is a view computed by survivorship rules — source trust tier, recency, corroboration count — never a stored truth.

Claim {
  entity_ref, field, value,
  evidence_id, extractor, confidence, observed_at
}

Two sources disagreeing produces a conflict record that surfaces in the interface rather than a silent overwrite. This one modelling choice is what makes evidence, scoring, conflict resolution, data-subject requests, and audit all fall out of the same table.

Citation validation

Every model verdict returns evidence_ids[] and quoted spans. A validator checks each ID exists in this run and each span appears in that evidence's extracted text — exact match first, normalized fuzzy match second. Failures are not retried into compliance; they are excluded from the score.

Content-addressed snapshots

Every fetch is stored keyed by SHA-256 of its body, with URL, HTTP status, robots decision, and fetch time. A score from three months ago can be re-audited against exactly the bytes that produced it — which is also what makes GDPR's “tell them where you got it” answerable per lead rather than per vendor.

Coverage, always visible

A lead scoring 82% on four of nine criteria is a different object from one scoring 82% on nine of nine. The interface must never conflate them, so every lead carries a coverage figure alongside its score.

Evidence also decays: a half-life per field class, because headcount from a fourteen-month-old page is not the same claim as headcount from last week, and the score should know that.

Scoring

Judgment and arithmetic, kept apart

Criterion {
  id, name,
  kind: hard_filter | weighted | bonus | disqualifier,
  weight,
  evaluator: structured_predicate | numeric_range | llm_judge,
  evidence_requirement
}
  • Hard filters and numeric ranges run as SQL — free, exact, no model involved.
  • LLM judges handle only genuinely fuzzy criteria: “sells into regulated healthcare”, “has invested in AI recently”. Each returns a verdict, confidence, evidence IDs, quoted spans, and a rationale.
  • Aggregation is deterministic arithmetic over those verdicts. The model never emits “87 out of 100”. A model that scores holistically cannot be tuned, audited, or explained.

Because judgment and aggregation are separate, user feedback tunes weights rather than prompts — explanations stay stable while ranking improves.

The cost lever: the rubric is byte-identical across every lead in a run. Put it in a cached prompt prefix and per-lead marginal cost collapses by roughly an order of magnitude. This is the concrete reason a rubric-plus-lead split beats one all-in-one agent prompt.

Entity resolution

Merging two real companies is worse than a duplicate

So merges are conservative and always reversible — clusters are recorded as links, never destructive overwrites. Ambiguous pairs land in a review queue instead of auto-merging.

Companies

The identity spine is the registered domain, but a domain alone breaks on rebrands, regional domains, holding structures, and acquisitions. The identifier ladder runs:

  • Strong — LEI, SEC CIK, Companies House number, verified primary domain
  • Medium — normalized legal name + country, canonical redirect target, social handles
  • Weak — trading name, embedding similarity of description

Blocking on normalized name tokens, domain root, and country keeps pairwise comparison tractable; vector similarity catches what token blocking misses.

People

Company plus normalized name, with verified work email as a strong key. Job changes make person records bitemporal — we track when a role was observed, not only what it is. Suppression and CRM dedup are hash-based, so a customer's existing contacts can be excluded without us ever storing their CRM in the clear.

Data sources

Tiered by permission posture

Current case law sets the line, and it is not where most people assume. hiQ v. LinkedIn established that scraping public pages does not violate the CFAA. Meta v. Bright Data (2024) declined to block logged-out scraping of public pages, reasoning that platform terms bind account holders rather than logged-out visitors. Reddit v. Perplexity (2025) then moved the risk to a different question entirely — circumvention of technical access controls under DMCA §1201.

So the line we draw is not “public versus private”. It is logged-out and un-circumvented versus everything else.

BOpen registries & government data M0: SEC EDGAR, GLEIF (CC0), USPTO, SAM.gov. Later: UK Companies House, EU registers Explicitly open. Highest trust tier for survivorship, and cheap. Start here.

Compliance

A subsystem, not a hardening pass

GDPR permits B2B prospecting under legitimate interest rather than opt-in, but conditions attach — and each maps to a component that has to exist from the start. Cumulative GDPR fines passed €7.1B by January 2026; this is not a thing to retrofit.

Message relevant to their professional rolePersona and rubric are role-scoped by construction
Transparency about where data came fromProvenance travels with every exported lead

Data minimization is a default, not a setting. Business-context fields only: work email, title, employer, public professional profile. Personal mobile numbers and home addresses are not collected — those are the fields that turn a B2B tool into a Clearview-shaped liability.

EU AI Act. B2B lead scoring is not an Annex III high-risk use case, so that regime does not attach — and the high-risk deadline was in any case deferred to December 2027 by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. Article 50 transparency duties remain live on the original schedule and would apply if outreach drafting is ever added.

Separation

Standalone from MOOR

The requirement is structural, and right now it is free. spartanapparelbiz-ui/leadmoor is an empty repository with zero commits, and no MOOR source is present in this environment. There is nothing to disentangle — only something to prevent from forming.

  • Separate repository, database, storage bucket, secrets, and deployment
  • Separate authentication — no shared identity provider, no shared session
  • A CI dependency check that fails the build on any import resolving to a MOOR package
  • If a utility is ever needed in both products, it is duplicated. Duplication is cheaper than coupling here
  • Any future integration goes over the public HTTP API and webhooks — the same contract a third party would use

One flag: the name. “LeadMoor” reads as a MOOR sub-product. If this needs to stand alone as a brand — separately positioned, sold, or owned — the name works against that. A rename costs nothing today and gets expensive after the first domain, customer, and contract.

Decisions

What was settled, and what it changes

Four questions were answered before implementation. None of them changes the architecture — every component above survives — but each sharply narrows what gets built first.

US-only for M0

Sources become SEC EDGAR, GLEIF, USPTO, and SAM.gov, plus search and first-party fetch. Companies House and the EU registers drop out and return when geography does.

Compliance gets lighter, not absent. CAN-SPAM replaces GDPR as the governing outreach regime. But roughly twenty US state privacy laws are in force by 2026, and while most carry a “publicly available information” exemption covering much of what Tier B and C return, deletion rights still attach — and the inferences we generate are ours, not exempt public record.

Keep in M0
  • Suppression service
  • Provenance on every claim
  • Deletion endpoint over the claim store
Defer to M2
  • Automated retention purge
  • Per-jurisdiction legal-basis records
  • Full DSAR workflow with identity verification

The first column is nearly free once claims exist, and expensive to retrofit. That asymmetry is the whole reason for the split.

Web-first, registries as the identity spine carries a real cost

No Tier A connector ships in M0. Two consequences follow, and the second is the one that matters.

Entity resolution carries more weight. With no provider handing us canonical IDs, the identifier ladder is the resolution strategy: SEC CIK and GLEIF LEI become the strong keys and verified primary domain does the rest. Workable — and exactly why Resolver is a port with a Splink escape hatch behind it.

Contact discovery is the genuinely hard part. Web-first company discovery is well-trodden; web-first people discovery is not. Honest sources are team and about pages, press releases, SEC officer filings, speaker lists, job postings, GitHub, and patent inventors. Names and roles are reachable that way. Verified work email frequently is not — and we have already ruled out guessing it from a pattern.

A lead is company + named person + role evidence. Work email is best-effort, marked unverified when it cannot be confirmed, and never fabricated.

If that proves too thin in practice, the narrow fix is a single Tier A connector used only for email resolution, sitting behind EnrichmentConnector like any other — a swappable dependency on one field rather than an abandonment of the stance. That call is better made with M0 evidence in hand than now.

Tens of leads, deep dossiers

This fits the evidence differentiator best, and it relaxes the cost pressure that shaped the scoring design.

  • Model tier moves up. At tens of leads per run, Opus for per-lead criterion judging is affordable and better; Sonnet becomes the fallback rather than the default.
  • Corroboration becomes a requirement. Any criterion driving a disqualification needs two independent sources — affordable at this volume, and a wrongly disqualified lead is otherwise invisible to the user.
  • Full rubric coverage per lead is the target, with coverage expected near 100% rather than merely reported.
  • The review queue is central, not an M1 nicety — a human sees every lead.

What M0 therefore does not need: bulk export tooling, aggressive prompt-cache optimization, the connector SDK, multi-tenant isolation, or the elaborate budget governor. A simple hard ceiling per run suffices.

LeadMoor retained

No action. Technical separation stands on its own through ADR-0001, and the positioning concern is recorded there should it change later.

Revised M0 definition of done. One real US request produces twenty to fifty leads, each with a named person, role evidence, full rubric coverage, and a dossier in which every field traces to a stored document and a byte range. Suppression and deletion work. Nothing in the run touched a Tier D source — because no such code path exists.
